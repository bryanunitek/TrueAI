# South Africa — National AI Framework

How the public corpus aligns with South Africa's AI regulatory framework
including the National AI Policy Framework, POPIA, and sector frameworks.

## The law

### 1. National AI policy

- **South African National AI Policy Framework** — Department of
  Communications and Digital Technologies; released for public comment
  2024; identifies priority application areas and governance principles.
- **AI Institute of South Africa (AIISA)** — established to coordinate
  national AI activity.
- **Presidential Commission on the Fourth Industrial Revolution** —
  earlier strategic framework recommending AI governance.

### 2. Data protection layer

- **Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)** — fully
  effective 1 July 2021.
- **Information Regulator** — supervisory authority.
- **POPIA Section 71** — addresses automated decision-making.

### 3. Cybersecurity layer

- **Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020** — primary cybercrime statute.
- **National Cybersecurity Policy Framework**.
- **SABRIC** — banking cybersecurity coordination.

### 4. Sector-specific frameworks

- **South African Reserve Bank (SARB)** — AI in financial services.
- **Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA)** — conduct regulation.
- **South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA)** — AI
  medical devices.
- **Independent Communications Authority of South Africa (ICASA)** —
  AI in telecommunications.

### 5. Equality framework

- **Constitution Section 9** — equality.
- **Promotion of Equality and Prevention of Unfair Discrimination Act 4
  of 2000 (PEPUDA)** — anti-discrimination.
- **Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998** — employment discrimination.
- **Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995** — labour rights.

## Provisions in scope and how the corpus fulfils each

### POPIA — Protection of Personal Information Act

POPIA provides:
- Eight conditions for lawful processing;
- Data subject rights;
- **Section 71 — Automated decision-making**: a data subject may not be
  subject to a decision based solely on automated processing intended
  to provide a profile of personal information that has legal
  consequences or substantially affects them.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: POPIA Section 71 is structurally
near-identical to GDPR Article 22. TrueAI Invariant 9 (Human
Sovereignty as Root) is incompatible with fully automated decisions in
the corpus's high-stakes contexts — the Reasonable Governance Threshold
routes such decisions to the named human in the Singular Pair.

### National AI Policy Framework

Sets principles including: human-centric; fair, inclusive, and ethical;
transparent and accountable; safe and secure; capacity-building;
sovereign control of data.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: Principles map directly onto Nine
Invariants and 12 Levels as covered in other jurisdictions.

### SARB + FSCA — financial sector AI

South African financial regulators have issued expectations on AI/ML
use including model risk and operational risk.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: Maps onto the same architectural
properties that satisfy SS1/23 in the UK and analogues.

### SAHPRA — AI medical devices

SAHPRA regulates AI as medical device.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: Same architectural mapping as German
MPDG, French HAS sections.

### PEPUDA + Employment Equity Act

Anti-discrimination provisions apply to AI affecting protected
characteristics.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: Invariants 4 and 6 structurally
prohibit discriminatory AI behaviour at architectural level.

### Cybercrimes Act — AI-enabled cybercrime

Cybercrimes Act applies to AI-enabled cybercrime including AI-generated
malware, deepfakes used for fraud, etc.

**How the corpus fulfils this**: TrueAI Invariant 4 (No Human
Influence) structurally prohibits deepfake-fraud failure modes.

## Honest gaps

   establishing.

   overlays.
